1. The Dual-Axis Economic Framework

Conventional economic discussions frequently conflate population definitions with adequacy standards. The Foundation strictly separates the two:

Axis 1 · The Population Anchor (Who are they?)

Defines the reference population by ranking individuals according to per-person household money income. The 30th percentile represents the boundary of the Bottom 30%.

Axis 2 · The Minimum Sustainable Living Cost (What does basic life cost?)

Models the gross money income required for an independent adult to maintain a minimally sustainable life without means-tested public assistance, debt-financing necessities, or shared household income.

The Time-Comparable Survival Gap (2024 Population Anchor − 2024 Living Cost) and Adequacy Ratio (2024 Population Anchor / 2024 Living Cost) evaluate economic margin strictly within matching temporal vintages.

2. Canonical Bottom-30 Population Anchor

Each person in a household receives an income value equal to total household money income divided by the number of persons supported:

per_person_household_income = HTOTVAL / H_NUMPER
  • Population Unit: Person. We rank humans, not households. Ranking households would give a one-person household the same weight as a six-person family.
  • Survey Weights: Evaluated using the official Census CPS ASEC March supplement person weight (MARSUPWT).
  • Percentile Convention: First observation where cumulative person weight reaches or exceeds 30% of total represented population weight.
  • No Equivalence Scale: We do not treat children or additional adults as fractional persons. One supported human counts as one human.

3. Minimum Sustainable Living Cost Architecture (`0.2.0-draft`)

Under Decision D-015, The Foundation retired the initial $27,960 synthesized national estimate and built a complete bottom-up county engine across all 50 states + DC.

4. Benchmark Comparisons & Methodological Divergences

The Foundation compares its model against external benchmark models, explaining all differences:

MIT Living Wage Calculator (~$42,500/year national single adult)

Divergence: MIT includes unsubsidized commercial employer healthcare rates, civic engagement, and local county cost aggregation. The Foundation models independent standard housing, USDA Low-Cost food, explicit auto ownership, unsubsidized Silver Marketplace healthcare, and full statutory taxes.

United For ALICE Survival Budget (~$31,200/year national single adult)

Divergence: ALICE includes a 10% contingency reserve but assumes smaller healthcare and transport baselines. The Foundation models full automobile ownership and unsubsidized Silver health insurance.

Official Poverty Measure ($15,650/year single adult)

Divergence: The OPM is based on a 1963 food-multiplier formula (3x food) and severely underestimates modern housing, healthcare, transit, and utility expenses.
